🌊 Why Choose Maryland Native Plants?

Maryland's native flora evolved over thousands of years alongside our local soils, intense summer humidity, and unique Chesapeake weather patterns. Converting even a small strip of lawn into a native garden bed reduces your weekend mowing time and creates a self-sustaining sanctuary that brings vibrant, fascinating wildlife right to your window.

🌱

Less Lawn to Mow & Water

Turf grass requires constant mowing, gas, and supplemental watering during hot August dry spells. Native perennials like Little Bluestem grass and Joe Pye Weed push roots 5–15 feet down into heavy Maryland clay, absorbing stormwater runoff and thriving on natural rainfall once established—while shallow native spreaders like Mountain Mint create dense surface mats that smother weeds without risking underground pipes.

🐦

Cool Birds & Pollinators Arrive

When you plant native nectar sources, nature responds immediately. Planting red tubular species like Cardinal Flower and Coral Honeysuckle attracts Ruby-throated Hummingbirds that return to feed at least twice a day throughout the summer season!

🦋

Essential Host Plants

90% of plant-eating insects are host specialists that can only reproduce on native species. Monarch caterpillars require Milkweed, Spicebush Swallowtails need Spicebush, and the Baltimore Checkerspot relies on White Turtlehead.

🛡️

Septic Line & Drain Field Safe

Worried about buried utilities or septic lines? Non-woody native perennials and grasses (like Little Bluestem, Joe Pye Weed, Black-Eyed Susan, and Mountain Mint) are completely safe to plant near or over drain fields! Unlike trees and invasive woody shrubs, herbaceous roots are soft and fibrous—they absorb excess water and prevent soil erosion without ever cracking or clogging underground pipes.

💡

Chesapeake Bay Impact: Traditional lawns require synthetic nitrogen fertilizers that wash into local storm drains during heavy summer thunderstorms, creating toxic algae blooms in the Bay. Native plants thrive naturally without synthetic chemical inputs!

🚀 Quick Start: Your Easy Lawn Conversion Guide

You don't need to dig up your whole yard overnight! Converting a small 4x4 pocket garden or a strip along your driveway creates an immediate pollinator sanctuary with minimal effort. Late summer through early fall is the ideal time to sheet mulch so cardboard and mulch smother grass and build soil over winter.

1

Mow & Cardboard Layer

Mow your selected turf grass as short as possible. Cover the grass completely with plain brown cardboard (remove tape and staples). Overlap edges by 6 inches so grass can't sneak through!

2

Mulch & Compost

Cover cardboard with 3–4 inches of hardwood mulch or leaf compost. Water thoroughly. The cardboard smothers weeds while breaking down into rich soil earthworms love.

3

Plant Native Starters

Cut small holes through the cardboard layer to plant your native perennials. Water weekly during their first summer until their deep roots establish!

🦌 Deer & Bunny Defense Guide

Protecting your native garden does not require turning your yard into a fortress! Understanding animal habits helps keep your plants safe.

💡 Early Season Repellent Strategy & Reality Check

Break Habits Early: White-tailed deer and cottontail rabbits establish their spring feeding routes in early April as new shoots first emerge. Spraying organic smell/taste repellents early in the season breaks their habit before your garden becomes part of their daily routine!

You Don't Need Everything: You don't have to build 8-foot fences or cover every plant! Aromatic screen plants (like Mountain Mint or Bergamot) naturally make garden beds unappealing to browse. Liquid smell repellents (putrescent egg/garlic) may smell strong for a couple of hours right after spraying, but once dry, they are completely unnoticeable to human noses while remaining highly effective against deer and rabbits for weeks.

🌿 Optional Companion Annuals for Border Screening:

While not native perennials, non-invasive garden herbs like Sweet Basil, Rosemary, and French Marigolds possess strong essential oils that deer and bunnies dislike. Planting them around tender young perennials adds an extra aromatic boundary.

Feature 🦌 White-Tailed Deer 🐇 Eastern Cottontail Rabbit
Damage Sign Ragged, torn leaves & shredded stems (deer lack upper incisors). Damage up to 6 feet high. Clean, sharp 45-degree angle cuts on tender stems under 2 feet tall. Chewed woody bark in winter.
Physical Barriers Requires 8-foot tall fencing or 3D double fencing to disrupt leap perception. Requires 1/2-inch hardware cloth mesh. Must be buried 2–3 inches deep so they cannot dig underneath!

💧 Hydrating the Sanctuary: Insect & Bird Water Stations

Maryland summers get hot and humid! Providing clean, safe water is just as important as supplying nectar for pollinators.

🐝 The Bee & Butterfly Puddle Station

Insects cannot swim in deep bird baths and will drown easily. Fill a shallow terracotta saucer with river pebbles, stones, or glass marbles. Add water so the stones remain half-exposed, giving bees and butterflies a stable landing pad to sip safely.

☀️ Solar Moving Water & Mosquito Prevention

Standing water breeds mosquitoes within 48 hours! Place a low-cost solar bubbler or fountain in your water dish. The moving water ripples keep mosquitoes from laying eggs while producing splashing sounds that lead migrating songbirds directly to your garden.
